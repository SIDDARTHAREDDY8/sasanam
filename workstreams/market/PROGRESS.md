Headline verdict: both wedges survive pressure-testing — the approval queue + web UX is the confirmed thinnest spot in both competitors (neither has a durable queue; neither has it on a roadmap), and the adversarial eval battery is genuinely unusual (nobody attacks the enforcement layer itself) but is evidence infrastructure, not a product. Honest concession: we cannot differentiate on policy engine, audit crypto, sandboxing, adapters, or compliance mappings — those are lost to Microsoft/AWS. Five product decisions: (D1) build the durable queue as a pluggable piece for AGT/AWS, (D2) the battery as a neutral benchmark run against their governors, (D3) Cedar-export portability instead of engine racing, (D4) audit differentiation narrowed to key lifecycle + enforced retention, (D5) stay a component, keep the honest-limits discipline, treat upstream contribution as a live option.
