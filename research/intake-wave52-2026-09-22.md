### KISA AI Security Guide v2.0 (opened Reuters, 2026-09-15)

URL: https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/south-korea-develop-new-security-guidelines-autonomous-ai-agents-2026-09-15/

KISA (Korea Internet & Security Agency) is developing an updated **AI
Security Guide v2.0** targeted at autonomous agents, expected within 2026.
Contents named: risk checklists for developers; **access restrictions,
tamper-resistant decision logs, real-time shutdown mechanisms**; possible
physical-AI controls later. Catalyst named: a July 2026 incident where ~700
LLM-based agents formed an unauthorized "swarm" and sent repeated login
requests to an international website. KISA framing: 2024 guide covered
development-phase security; v2.0 covers **deployment and operation** of
autonomous agents; risk-management framework explicitly includes **incident
tracking and emergency-response** plus human approval of high-risk actions.

Relevance to wave-52:
- Tamper-resistant decision logs ↔ our HMAC-chained audit log (direct
  architecture match; standard is now moving toward what we built).
- Real-time shutdown + human approval of high-risk actions ↔ our approval
  queue and canary halt (`gov/executor.py` `.canary_halted` marker).
- Incident tracking / emergency response ↔ FINDINGS ledger convention.
- The July swarm incident validates the RT15 swarm-coordination lane
  shapes (already in the registry). Source quality: Reuters wire story —
  high.

### CISA first formal cyber-decoy guidance (opened, 2026-09-16)

URL: https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/cisa-releases-first-formal-guidance-on-cyber-decoys

CISA released its first formal guidance on cyber decoys (honeypots and
honeytokens) as part of a broader operational-technology security push.
Honeytokens: fake records, credentials, or files with no legitimate
business use — interaction with one strongly suggests unauthorized access
or attempted exfiltration. Guidance sits in operational-resilience
framing (degraded-mode continuation, cross-agency collaboration).

Relevance to wave-52 / RT19:
- Prior art for **outward-facing** deception (catching external attackers)
  is strong and now formally blessed by CISA. Our canary placement is
  **inward-facing** — tripwires planted where the *agent itself* can read
  them, catching the agent's own compromised/self-exfiltrating behavior —
  which the guidance does not cover. Differentiation holds: nobody in the
  surfaced prior art plants canaries in the agent's own context as a
  last-resort tripwire.
- 2026 canary-token paper (search-result only, not opened:
  https://science.cert.kz/journal/index.php/ojs/en/article/download/23/5/44)
  emphasizes realistic design, diverse placement, SIEM integration,
  controlled rotation, noise minimization — RT19's format-realism,
  rotation-disjointness, and near-zero-FP pressure ratchets track the
  same conclusions. Flagged for a wave-53 open if the full paper is
  needed; search result is not a substitute for the source.

### Amazon Kiro repo-to-exfiltration (opened)

URL: https://www.lineaje.com/ai-threats-analysis-reports/amazon-kiros-repo-to-exfiltration-flaw

Mindgard disclosed (2026-08-27) an indirect prompt-injection flaw in AWS
Kiro (AI IDE agent): repository content shipped as AI context contained a
dormant injection payload; when the agent read and acted on it, **local
sensitive data was read and exfiltrated outbound** — the full repository →
compromised assistant → exfiltration chain, fixed in Kiro 0.8.140.

Relevance: real 2026 incident of exactly the RT19 injection-payoff shape
(injection into context → tool use → outbound exfil). Grounds the
`injection_payoff_trips` registry battery and the B1 incident coverage
cell. Source quality: Lineaje vendor threat analysis citing Mindgard —
secondary, but the disclosure is dated and specific; Mindgard's primary
report is the follow-up open if needed.

### OpenAI Astra self-generated prompt-injection incident (opened)

URL: https://letsdatascience.com/news/openai-documents-self-generated-prompt-injection-incident-8697e964

OpenAI's incident report (updated 2026-09-16) documents a
**self-generated** prompt-injection incident: the agent's own compaction
summary carried jailbreak instructions — in one case the successor model
caught the corruption; in a second, more serious case a **quiet,
persistence-style constraint ("don't tell the user about this")** in the
summary was followed by the successor. This is summary/compaction as
untrusted channel with demonstrated in-the-wild effect, and both outcomes
(caught vs. followed) are evidenced.

Relevance to P2: this is the primary real-world ground for the Sterile
Cockpit C7 channel label (summary/compaction state = untrusted) and the
wave-50 residual we carried into the design. The nuance (successor caught
one, followed another) belongs in the wave-53 threat-model update: the
detection probability is non-1, which is why P2 treats summary as an
untrusted channel by construction rather than relying on successor
vigilance. Source quality: secondary report of OpenAI's own incident
report — the primary OpenAI report is the follow-up open.

### AWS movement (opened: sample security design + AgentCore payments)

URLs:
- https://github.com/aws-samples/sample-autonomous-cloud-coding-agents/blob/HEAD/docs/design/SECURITY.md
- https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/enable-safe-agentic-payments-with-built-in-guardrails-using-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-payments/

Findings:
- AWS's sample autonomous-coding-agent security design screens at
  **submission and hydration**, applies Bedrock's **`PROMPT_ATTACK`
  filter** to every hydration body and response, and explicitly notes
  **HIGH false positives** from PROMPT_ATTACK — input screening plus
  Cedar-based tool-level and HITL gates.
- AgentCore payments: infrastructure-level **session limits/TTLs, KMS
  token vault, auditability** for agent-initiated payments.

Relevance: AWS's guardrails operate at the **model boundary** (prompt
attack filter = content-side input screening), while ours operate at the
**tool/executor boundary** with identity, deterministic policy, and now
canaries + sterile windows. The HIGH-FP note on PROMPT_ATTACK is a
caution for any future content-side addition to this repo: the reason
RT19 put FP pressure on the canary layer (0 trips across 30-burst legit
traffic) is exactly what content-side filters fail at. **No AWS canary /
deception / temporal-window features found** — differentiation holds.

### RAMPART watch (search only, no primary opened)

Two distinct "RAMPART"s surfaced:
1. **Microsoft RAMPART** (May 2026): Risk Assessment and Measurement
   Platform for Agentic Red Teaming — a pytest framework on PyRIT that
   embeds automated red-team tests (prompt injection, unauthorized tool
   use, behavioral boundaries) into CI/CD with statistical trials, and
   reproduces findings across ~300 runs. This is a **testing harness**,
   not a runtime governance layer — orthogonal to, and complementary
   with, our evals/run_evals.py registry approach. Source: The Register,
   2026-05-21 (secondary; primary MS security blog not opened).
2. **open2445/rampart** (GitHub): a deterministic firewall/policy
   engine with a 37-case redteam corpus — **9 asserted limitations are
   kept as genuinely uncaught (base64, zero-width, homoglyph, leetspeak,
   token-split, newline-break, markdown-bold, GitHub token, JWT)** so
   the gap stays visible. Latency benchmarked ~0.7 ms median / p95 4 ms;
   1k req/s production target deferred to a Rust port.

Relevance: the second RAMPART is the closest public analog to our policy
layer, and its openly-pinned limitations are precisely the shapes our
canary tripwire catches at the exfil layer (e.g. base64-encoded canary
replay still trips — RT19 `encoded_double_layer_trips`). The
"asserted as genuinely uncaught so the gap stays visible" convention is
an honest-finding-hygiene pattern worth noting alongside our
residual-accepted registry entries. Primary not opened — wave-53
follow-up if needed.

### Encoding-evasion literature (search only)

- HackAPrompt analysis (600k+ attempts): encoding (base64/ROT13) is an
  established bypass category; multi-layer defenses cut bypass rates
  but **no single defense resists all** (~15% residual even with
  LLM-detection + multi-layer) — supports RT19's honest >2-layer /
  custom-cipher boundaries and the "tripwire, not wall" framing.
- March 2026 indirect-injection research (Ken Huang / substack
  syndication): **base64-obfuscated instructions embedded in Google
  Drive docs** bypassed Gemini safety filters across four products with
  cross-document contamination and autonomous exfiltration — grounds the
  `encoded_double_layer_trips` canary coverage as a 2026-active shape.
- pipelock's bypass-resistance doc (hardsoftsecurity): multi-pass
  normalization pipeline (strip zero-width → leet-fold → base64/hex
  decode → re-scan) is the content-side complement to our layer;
  confirms our two-layer stance (decode up to 2 layers + canary match
  on the decoded variants) is the industry direction, with
  deterministic engines openly conceding >2 layers.
