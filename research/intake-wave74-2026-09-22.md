## What the intake establishes (load-bearing for the wave-74 design)

### South et al. three-token shape — CONFIRMED as the adopted architecture
- The paper's framework: **user ID tokens, agent-ID tokens, and
  delegation tokens** (arXiv:2501.09674, ICML 2025). The delegation
  token "explicitly binds the User, the Agent, and the Scope into a
  single, verifiable artifact" and carries **verifiable references
  (e.g., hashes or identifiers) to both the User's ID-token claims and
  the Agent-ID token claims**, alongside scope, validity conditions,
  and **audit URLs**.
- Our implementation matches field-for-field: `user_token_hash` /
  `agent_token_hash` (SHA-256 refs, South's "verifiable references"),
  `scope`, `expires_at`, `revocation_endpoint`, `audit_urls`. The
  design choice to put **hashes, not claims, in the binding** is
  South's, not ours — and it is what keeps full tokens out of the
  agent-reachable log.

### MongoDB Pattern 4 (A2A authority chaining) — the two break points, pinned
- "Each handoff produces a signed token that **records the full chain
  back to the originating user**, **narrows the scope at every hop**
  so each downstream agent can do strictly less than the one before,
  and lets **every recipient verify the chain rather than trust it**."
- "Even implemented correctly, the chain breaks at the two points it
  exists to protect: **a hop that does not actually narrow scope
  re-broadens authority** instead of reducing it, and **a recipient
  that trusts the chain's claims rather than verifying its signatures**
  defeats the purpose of signing them."
- These are exactly the two properties the wave-74 red-team battery
  pins: `scope_escalation_refused` (issuance-time AND chain-verify-time
  monotonicity) and the verify-not-trust chain walk in
  `verify_delegation_chain` / `parent_signature_swap_refused`.

### MongoDB Pattern 1 — the permission intersection, stated independently
- "An action should proceed only when the **requester's permissions,
  the agent's permissions, and the tool's permissions all allow it**,
  and that conjunction has to be **available where the call is actually
  made**."
- Independent confirmation of the effective-permission conjunction
  (user ∩ agent ∩ delegation scope) AND of its placement: enforced at
  the call point in code (the executor gate), not merely expressed in
  policy text. "Lose the originating identity at the first hop, and
  the downstream system can no longer evaluate it" — the reason the
  gate binds `session_id`/`run_id` and the acting `agent_id`.

### SPIFFE two-layer split — CONFIRMED with the exact layer wording
- Red Hat (Jun 2026): "SPIFFE identities operate at two different
  layers. At the **transport layer**, SPIRE issues X.509 certificates
  to *services*... At the **application layer**, *users* and *agents*
  are identified by logical identifiers carried inside JWT tokens."
  - "Transport layer (mTLS): **WHO is calling?** → Service identity"
  - "Application layer (JWT): **ON BEHALF of whom?** → User + agent
    delegation chain"
- The 1-hour SVID TTL in their demo is the grounding for our
  `DEFAULT_TTL_S = 3600` (already documented in code).
- dyologician/a1 states the composition explicitly: "**SPIRE for
  workload identity (mTLS between services) and A1 for human → agent →
  sub-agent delegation chains inside those workloads**" — the same
  two-layer split our tree implements (F55 provisioned session identity
  + process boundary as transport; three-token chain as application).
- Honest statement preserved: this tree has no live SPIFFE/mTLS; the
  transport layer here is the F55 provisioned identity + process
  boundary. The intake does not change that — it confirms the split is
  the industry-agreed shape, which is why the design doc states the
  local realization explicitly rather than claiming mTLS.

### Revocation seam — industry direction noted
- Agentic Fabriq's survey notes the WIMSE draft reaching for the
  **OpenID Shared Signals Framework for revocation** and
  `draft-ietf-oauth-identity-chaining` for cross-domain cases. No
  turnkey revocation propagation exists; our stated boundary (TTL-only
  default, checker seam, true propagation out of scope) matches the
  industry state, not just our constraints.

### What the intake did NOT change
- Signature choice stays Ed25519 (no source suggested HMAC for the
  human-signature property; the unforgeability-by-verifier argument
  stands).
- Scope language stays v1 tool-granular (South's "standardized scope
  definitions" is listed as *future research*, confirming v1 is the
  honest scope).
- No fourth token shape: South's paper describes exactly three; the
  v2 provenance slot validator's rejection of extra fields is the
  enforcement of that.
