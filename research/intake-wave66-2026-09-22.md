## Applied learnings (2)

1. **Rate-limit the expensive sink, keep the events inspectable
   elsewhere — and make the limit itself visible.** Broadcom's EDR
   docs (from the same search): rate limiting "limits the incident
   creation rate ... the events that create the incidents still exist
   and you can still investigate them at any time. You are not
   preventing events from being logged." IBM: "monitor to ensure that
   any limits, such as quota, aren't reached." Our wave-66 design is
   the same shape applied to a hash-chained log: exhaustion refuses
   LOUD at the drain (the expensive sink), the undrained records stay
   in the sink (peek-confirm — inspectable, nothing silently
   dropped), and the refusal is itself chained as
   `unvetted_ack_cumulative_budget_hit` — the "monitor so limits
   aren't silently reached" mechanism. Rejects the silent-withhold
   alternative for the same reason the industry rejects silent
   incident suppression.
2. **Backpressure, not silent drop, is the industry fix for export
   buffer overflow.** A SIEM-monitoring runbook in the results:
   "High event volume fills the export buffer. Fix: Implement
   backpressure and batch exports." Our refuse-loud
   (`AckCumulativeBudgetExceeded`, fail-closed per F22) is
   backpressure at the drain: the producer (ack volume) is told NO
   loudly instead of the evidence being quietly shed. Also
   validates the per-batch + cumulative two-tier structure: the
   per-sink budget is the "batch export" tier, the cumulative cap is
   the run-level backpressure tier.
